\subsection{Error Quantification}

\subsubsection{Per-source proxy errors}

\autoref{fig:simple_major_source} shows the relative error of estimating GMSLC from the integral average of SSHC over the altimetry-observable ocean for each major ice sheet independently.

At the standard altimetry latitude range of $60$--$75^\circ$ \autocite{lickleyBiasEstimatesGlobal2018}, the GIS contribution is underestimated by 5.8--8.2\%, the EAIS contribution is underestimated by 0--2\%, and the WAIS contribution ranges from a 2.3\% overestimation to a 0.5\% underestimation. The GIS and EAIS errors increase with the latitude of the altimetry boundary.

\subsection{Sensitivity tests}

\notebookcite{thomasholland07InversionSensitivityipynb2026}

\autoref{fig:sensitivity-z_score} shows the posterior z-scores across all sensitivity cases. In every case, the posterior contains the true GMSL value and shows no large biases across variants, demonstrating that the Bayesian inversion is robust to prior misspecification. Spatial recovery across all cases is shown in the supplementary figures (\autoref{fig:sensitivity-true-inversion} to \autoref{fig:sensitivity-variance}).

The three parameter groups differ in character: length scale affects spatial structure but has little effect on GMSL recovery; mean offset tests a worst-case scenario where the true value lies outside the prior support, and has small effects on both GMSL recovery but the recovery is still good; and covariance amplitude has the most practical implications, with higher variance cases being poorly conditioned and requiring substantially more conjugate gradient iterations to converge (47, 197, and 610 iterations for multipliers of 0.5, 2.0, and 5.0 respectively). The prior mean offset and length scale have comparatively little effect on convergence behaviour.

\subsection{Joint inversion using ice, firn, and ocean dynamic changes}

\autoref{fig:joint-inversion} shows the results of a joint inversion for ice thickness change, firn thickness change, and ocean dynamic changes from a synthetic test using ocean altimetry, ice altimetry, and GRACE data. The posterior expectation captures the spatial structure of ice and firn thickness change well. However, some regions show lower recovery of firn thickness change.
