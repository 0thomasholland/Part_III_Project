Previous approaches investigating ice-driven sea level change (SLC) either neglect the full physics of gravitationally self-consistent SLC, or impose prior constraints on ice geometry as hard, external judgements rather than probabilistic beliefs. The SSH proxy method exemplifies the first problem and the discrete parameterisation approach of \textcite{coulsonInvertingSeaSurface2026} illustrates the second. The infinite-dimensional Bayesian framework of \textcite{heathcoteScaleableBayesianFramework2026} implemented in this report provides a solution to both problems, and the results presented demonstrate that it does so in a way that is scientifically meaningful and computationally tractable.

The error quantification analysis demonstrates that the altimetry based SSHC proxy for GMSLC introduces source-dependent biases. GMSLC calculations are biased towards underestimation of approximately 5.8--8.2\% for GIS melt or 0.5--2\% for EAIS and over estimations of approximately 1--2\% for WAIS. This source-dependence itself precludes any scalar correction; the bias depends on where the ice is melting, and correcting for it requires knowledge of the very quantity being estimated. The Bayesian inversion result shows that replacing the proxy with a full inversion substantially improves posterior calibration: 60.5\% of synthetic twin experiments fall within one posterior standard deviation compared to 14.6\% under the proxy method (n=261), a result that constitutes direct empirical evidence that the framework produces well-calibrated uncertainties rather than merely plausible ones. The joint inversion over ice thickness change, firn compaction, and ocean dynamic topography extends this further: knock-out tests demonstrate that each data type contributes non-redundant information, and the posterior successfully disentangles contributions that are entangled at the level of the observations.

This is a qualitative advance over both the proxy approach and the finite-dimensional parameterisation approach, which treats ocean dynamic corrections as a preprocessing step and thereby severs the statistical coupling between signals that a joint inference should exploit. The sensitivity tests indicate that the method is robust to prior mis-specification, although the computational cost of the inference is sensitive to the choice of prior covariance amplitude, which suggests that careful calibration of this aspect of the prior is important for computational efficiency but not strictly necessary for scientific reasons.

Taken together, these results demonstrate that application of the infinite-dimensional Bayesian framework of \textcite{heathcoteScaleableBayesianFramework2026} provides three things that neither the SSHC proxy nor the discrete parameterisation approach can offer simultaneously: firstly principled uncertainty propagation through the full forward physics; secondly, spatially correlated posteriors of ice change with structure that is inherited from the prior and the data jointly, rather than through a discretisation scheme; and lastly joint inference over competing signals with formal uncertainty quantification on the trade-offs between them. The Richards curve ice prior developed here adds a further element: rather than encoding physical knowledge as a hard geometric constraint, it encodes it as a spatially varying belief about the plausibility of mass change, conditioned on ice thickness.

\textcite{coulsonInvertingSeaSurface2026} does, however, provide the natural complement to these synthetic results as they provide a proof-of-concept inversion against real altimetry. They demonstrate that the SSH fingerprint of GIS melt is recoverable from satellite observations when appropriate corrections are applied, as was suggested in \textcite{coulsonDetectionSeaLevel2022}. That work establishes the observational basis, and the framework developed here provides improved machinery; combining the two would give a substantially more reliable estimate of ice mass flux. A preliminary test against the \textcite{copernicusclimatechangeserviceGLOBALOCEANGRIDDED2021} dataset is encouraging and consistent with independent estimates, though a full application requires the data corrections and systematic validation that lie beyond the scope of this report.

Several directions for future work follow from the results. The sensitivity of the joint inversion to prior choices requires investigation through prior sensitivity tests. Also, how the spatial structure of the priors could lead to changes in sensitivity and recovery of the joint inversion is an open question. More broadly, the composite model space formulation is not limited to the three components considered here: the thermosteric and halosteric contributions to ocean dynamic topography identified by \textcite{heathcoteScaleableBayesianFramework2026} could be incorporated, offering a route toward a more complete accounting of the SL budget within the same inferential framework that could be applicable across many domains. The methods developed within this report and within \textcite{heathcoteScaleableBayesianFramework2026} using \citetitle{al-attarPySLFPPythonSea2025} provide the foundation for a wide range of future work.
