\subsection{Error of using SSHC as a proxy for SLC in GMSL estimation}

The key result from the per-source error characterisation is that the proxy error differs in both sign and magnitude across ice sources: Greenland melt leads to a systematic underestimation of GMSL, while West Antarctic sources produce a smaller bias of variable sign. This source dependence means that the SSHC proxy error cannot be corrected by a single scalar factor; the correction would itself depend on the unknown spatial distribution of ice loss \autocite{lickleyBiasEstimatesGlobal2018, al-attarReciprocitySensitivityKernels2023}.

At the global scale the resulting error is modest, on the order of 1\%; however, regional averages exhibit substantially larger and spatially variable biases, since SSHC and SLC differ by the solid-earth deformation and geoid perturbation terms, which are spatially heterogeneous functions of the ice load distribution. This motivates a more principled inference framework in which the full fingerprint physics is incorporated into the estimation procedure.


\subsection{Inversion for ice thickness change from ocean altimetry}

The improved $z$-scores from the Bayesian inversion suggest it is more effective at recovering the true GMSL contribution from ice melt than the old method, likely because it correctly accounts for the non-uniform spatial structure of the sea level fingerprints associated with different ice sources. The old method's bias and poor uncertainty quantification likely arise from its reliance on a simpler approach that does not capture the non-uniform spatial patterns of sea level change, causing systematic underestimation and overconfidence. Critically, the new method also incorporates other sources of SLC, such as ODT, which in the old method would require corrections with poorly quantified errors.

\subsection{Joint inversion using ice, firn, and ocean dynamic changes}

The inversions show good recovery of the detail and good sensitivity when using all data types. Knock-out tests show that there is information gained from the data types, specifically ice altimetry constraining the trade-off between the different densities. The supplementary figure shows that SSH altimetry is critical for constraining ODT, which makes intuitive sense as it provides the spatial coverage needed to separate ocean dynamic signals from ice-driven fingerprints. This is supported by a preliminary sensitivity test before the implementation of GRACE data which showed no recovered constraints on ocean dynamics without SSH altimetry.

The loss in ocean dynamic topography is acceptable, given the altimetry noise parameter (1\,mm standard deviation) and so only recovering features over this noise level is expected.
