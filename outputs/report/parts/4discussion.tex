\subsection{Error of using SSHC as a proxy for SLC in GMSL estimation}

The key result from this analysis is that the proxy error differs in both sign and magnitude across ice sources: Greenland melt leads to a systematic underestimation of GMSL, while West Antarctic sources produce a smaller bias of variable sign. This source dependence means that the SSHC proxy error cannot be corrected by a single scalar factor, the correction would itself depend on the unknown spatial distribution of ice loss.

at the global scale the resulting error is modest, on the order of 1\%, however regional averages exhibit substantially larger and spatially variable biases, reflecting the fact that SSHC and SLC differ by the solid-earth deformation and geoid perturbation terms that are spatially heterogeneous functions of the ice load distribution. This motivates a more principled inference framework in which the full fingerprint physics is incorporated into the estimation procedure.


\subsection{Inversion for ice thickness change from ocean altimetry}

This improved performance of the Bayesian inversion method suggests that it is more effective at recovering the true GMSL contribution from ice melt compared to the old method, likely because it correctly accounts for the non-uniform spatial structure of the sea level fingerprints associated with different ice sources. The old method's bias and poor uncertainty quantification may be due to its reliance on a simpler approach that does not fully capture the complex spatial patterns of sea level change driven by ice melt, leading to systematic underestimation and overconfidence in its estimates. Critically, the new method is able to build in other sources of SLC, such as ODT, which would require approximations or corrections for in the old method which would have poorly quantified errors.
