\subsection{Error Quantification}

The objective of this section is to quantify the systematic error introduced by using sea surface height change (SSHC) as a proxy for sea level change (SLC) when estimating global mean sea level (GMSL) from satellite altimetry. This error arises because SSHC and SLC differ by the solid-earth deformation and geoid perturbation terms, which are spatially heterogeneous functions of the ice load distribution. We first characterise the error for known ice sources, and finally generalise to the case where the source distribution is itself uncertain.

In the quantification of altimetry error, we use the limit outlined in \autoref{eq:altimetry_equation}, which results in the altimetery being a continuous integral of SSHC. By framing the error analysis in terms of this continuous average rather than a specific satellite configuration, the resulting error bounds are independent of the particular sampling geometry and reflect a fundamental lower bound on the approximation error inherent to the method itself.

\subsubsection{Per-source error characterisation}

\notebookcite{thomasholland02MajorSourcesipynb2026}

To build physical intuition for the proxy error, we first consider the case where the ice source is known. Uniform melt scenarios are constructed independently for each of the three major ice sheets: Greenland (\textbf{GIS}), West Antartic (\textbf{WAIS}), and East Antartic (\textbf{EAIS}), normalised to produce 1~mm of true GMSL change. For each scenario, the ice load is propagated through operators to yield the corresponding SLC and SSHC fields:

\begin{align}
    \slc &= \operatorslc \circ \operatorloadice (\thicknesschangeice) \\
    \sshc &= \operatorsshc \circ \operatorslc \circ \operatorloadice (\thicknesschangeice)
\end{align}

GMSL is then estimated for SSHC (\autoref{eq:gmsl_ssh_alt_integral}), and the percentage error relative to the true mass-balance GMSL (\autoref{eq:gmsl_integral}) can be computed:

\begin{align}
    \gmslctrue &= \operatorgmsl{\text{true}} \circ \operatorslc \circ \operatorloadice (\thicknesschangeice),\\
    \gmslcalt &= \operatorgmsl{\text{alt}} \circ \operatorsshc \circ \operatorslc \circ \operatorloadice (\thicknesschangeice),\\
    \text{error} &= \frac{\gmslcalt - \gmslctrue}{\gmslctrue} \times 100\%.
\end{align}


As both SLC and SSHC are linear functions of the ice load, the resulting error is independent of the magnitude of the ice change, and thus generalises to any scenario with the same spatial pattern of mass loss.

By varying the latitudinal extent of the altimetry domain, the dependence of this error on altimetry coverage can be traced for each ice sheet independently. This serves both to characterise the source-dependent structure of the proxy bias and to cross-validate against the results of \textcite{lickleyBiasEstimatesGlobal2018}, who demonstrated a similar latitude dependence using a different framework.

\subsubsection{Error under source uncertainty}

In practice, the spatial distribution of ice loss is not known a priori, and so the proxy error inherits this uncertainty. To quantify the error in this more realistic setting, a synthetic ice sheet change scenario is constructed as per \autoref{sec:ice_thickness_priors}, representing our prior uncertainty about the spatial pattern of mass loss. This measure is propagated through the sea level fingerprint operator to yield distributions over both the true SLC field and its corresponding SSHC field.
